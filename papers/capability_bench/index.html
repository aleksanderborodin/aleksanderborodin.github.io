01

Abstract

Deploying LLM-driven embodied agents in real homes requires more than following one-shot instructions in a reset simulator. A useful household robot has to remember what happened earlier, react to a world that keeps changing around it, and act at the right moment in continuous time.

We introduce LivingGrid, a persistent household simulator with environment-driven transitions and intent-driven human routines. On top of it, CapabilityBench organizes tasks along a continuous timeline and evaluates four sources of difficulty: foundational reasoning, memory, dynamic adaptation, and temporal action.

04

System overview

LivingGrid provides the physical world. CapabilityBench schedules and grades the cognitive pressure placed on the robot.

LivingGrid

A world that runs without the robot

Rooms, assets, surfaces, objects, and agents form a semantic scene graph over a traversable grid. World transitions and human routines continue in fixed time ticks.

CapabilityBench

Scenarios that share consequences

Milestone-based tasks are scheduled along one timeline. The action boundary stays public and stable, allowing policies and planning wrappers to be compared fairly.

05

Capability suite

The paper groups scenarios by the source of reasoning difficulty, producing an interpretable profile rather than a single leaderboard number.

F6 scenarios

Foundational

Instruction grounding, state change, tool use, delivery, and long-horizon decomposition.

M3 scenarios

Memory

Information must survive simulated time gaps and intervening experience.

D4 scenarios

Dynamic

The agent re-grounds and repairs its plan when people or objects change.

T2 scenarios

Temporal

The right action depends on a clock time, ordering constraint, or world event.
